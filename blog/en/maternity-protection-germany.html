German Maternity Protection 2026: Schutzfrist, Maternity Allowance and Employer Subsidy Explained

May 7, 2026 · 11 min read

German maternity protection (Mutterschutz) is one of the strongest legal protections in Europe. It guarantees expectant and new mothers full income replacement and a paid break around birth. The maternity protection calculator shows your protection period, the allowance from your health insurance and the employer subsidy — in seconds.

How Long Is the Maternity Protection Period?

The Schutzfrist under §3 MuSchG covers two phases: 6 weeks before birth and 8 weeks after. In special cases the post-birth phase extends to 12 weeks.

Phase Standard birth Multiple / premature / disabled
Before birth6 weeks (42 days)6 weeks (42 days)
After birth8 weeks (56 days)12 weeks (84 days)
Total14 weeks (98 days)18 weeks (126 days)

During this period a statutory employment ban applies — with full income replacement.

Who Pays What During Maternity?

Two sources combine to fully replace the average net pay of the last three months: the statutory health insurance and the employer.

Maternity Allowance (§19 MuSchG, §24i SGB V)

Women in the statutory health insurance receive maternity allowance from their fund — capped at €13 per calendar day (about €390/month). This allowance is tax-free and not subject to social-security contributions.

Employer Subsidy (§20 MuSchG)

The employer tops up the difference between €13/day and the average daily net pay of the last three months. Example: at €2,500 net per month (≈ €83.33/day) the employer pays €70.33 per day — about €2,110 per month.

Privately Insured and Self-Employed Women

Women with private health insurance do not receive maternity allowance from a statutory fund. Instead they get a one-off payment of up to €210 from the Federal Office for Social Security — plus the full employer subsidy. Self-employed women rely on private daily-sickness benefit policies.

Transition to Elterngeld (Parental Allowance)

After the protection period ends, parental allowance (Elterngeld) usually kicks in. Plan both phases together:

  • During Mutterschutz: Full net replacement (health insurance + employer)
  • After Mutterschutz: 65–67 % of net pay as Elterngeld (12 + 2 partner months)

Tip: Elterngeld is calculated from the net pay before birth. Switching to a more favourable tax class at least 7 months before the start of maternity protection can substantially raise the parental allowance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I waive the protection period before birth?

Yes — voluntarily, and only before birth. You must declare this in writing and may revoke at any time. The 8-week post-birth protection cannot be waived.

What happens with a premature birth?

If the baby arrives before the due date, the unused days from the pre-birth phase are added to the post-birth phase — which itself is extended to 12 weeks.

Do I get full pay during a doctor-ordered employment ban?

Yes. A doctor-ordered ban outside the standard Schutzfrist obliges the employer to pay full Mutterschutzlohn (§18 MuSchG). The health insurance reimburses the employer through the U2 levy.

Common Mistakes

  • Applying for maternity allowance too late: Apply about 7 weeks before the due date with a doctor's certificate.
  • Forgetting the tax-class switch: The change must be in place at least 7 months before the start of maternity protection to affect Elterngeld.
  • Underestimating private insurance gaps: Privately insured women should review their daily-sickness coverage early.
  • Not updating the due date: If the due date shifts, the Schutzfrist shifts too.

Conclusion

Maternity protection in Germany delivers full financial coverage — but only if you know the deadlines and plan properly. Calculate early what your health insurance and employer pay, and plan the transition into Elterngeld.

Mutterschutz is more than an employment ban — it is a full income-replacement system with double coverage.
